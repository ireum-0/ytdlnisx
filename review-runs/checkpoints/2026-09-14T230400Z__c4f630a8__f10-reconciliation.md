## Remaining same-root P2 — exact successor ownership can remain volatile

When exact successor debt persistence fails, `scheduleSuccessor()` passes the exact successor as `fallbackDebt` to `ensureReplayOwnerLocked()` and returns failure to the worker. That fallback is process-local only.

`CleanUpLeftoverDownloads` still has a finite WorkManager retry budget for successor-publication failure. Therefore repeated debt-persistence failure can reach the final worker attempt while the exact successor has no durable pending-debt tuple and no WorkRequest. If the process dies before the process-local replay owner succeeds in persisting that fallback, the exact successor occurrence identity is not reconstructible from durable state. Startup can derive a later occurrence from current time, but that is not the exact already-owned successor occurrence required by the F10 chain invariant.

Required correction: a worker occurrence must not become terminal while its exact successor responsibility exists only in volatile process memory. Before terminalization, responsibility must be represented by a durable/reconstructible carrier across process death, or the current durable WorkManager occurrence itself must remain the recoverable owner until the exact successor responsibility is safely published.

## Stronger combined frontier — stale persisted predecessor debt shadows newer fallback successor

The final source also leaves an exact stale-debt collision:

1. `clearDebtIfCurrent()` removes matching pending debt with `SharedPreferences.Editor.commit()` but ignores the returned success value and then unconditionally calls `stopReplayOwnerLocked()`.
2. Thus an accepted predecessor occurrence D1 can remain durably recorded as pending debt if that clear commit fails, while its replay owner is nevertheless retired.
3. D1 later completes and computes exact successor D2.
4. If persistence of D2 debt also fails, `scheduleSuccessor()` asks `ensureReplayOwnerLocked(context, fallbackDebt = D2)` to retain D2 in process memory.
5. `ensureReplayOwnerLocked()` chooses `readSchedulingDebt(preferences) ?: fallbackDebt`; the stale persisted D1 debt therefore wins over the newer D2 fallback.
6. Even if a D2 replay were started independently, `isSchedulingDebtCurrent(D2)` returns false whenever any different persisted debt exists, so stale D1 prevents D2 replay from owning recovery.
7. After D1 becomes terminal, reconciliation can still see the stale D1 debt as the persisted recovery occurrence and may re-publish D1 instead of publishing exact successor D2.

This violates one-logical-schedule/exact-occurrence ownership and can duplicate/replay a completed predecessor while losing its intended successor.

Required correction:

- durable debt clear/removal must not retire recovery ownership unless the durable transition actually succeeded or an equivalent exact scheduler state safely carries the same responsibility;
- a stale predecessor debt must never shadow a newer exact successor responsibility;
- process restart must reconstruct the exact current successor responsibility;
- repeated persistence failures must not permit finite worker terminalization to discard the exact successor;
- disable/supersession must still fence obsolete generations rather than causing unbounded retries.
